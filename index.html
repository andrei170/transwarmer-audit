Pre-Call AuditForty Hours To Ten
Pre-Call Audit · Transwarmer Conservatories Ltd

You Want A Ten Hour Week. Nothing On Your Site Is Built To Give You One.

Prepared for Andrew Perridge · 4 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
What You Told Our Team
The brief, as it reached us
  • You are the sole decision maker in the business.
  • One to three clients a week from word of mouth and referrals — and usually the lower end of that.
  • Average job £10,000 to £12,000.
  • Three to five people currently, and you want three more who are quality and committed.
  • The staffing has been like this for a few years. Not committed, and without the skillset you need.
  • ⭐ And the actual goal: to get from a forty hour week down to somewhere between one and ten.

⭐⭐ That last line is the brief, and the other five are symptoms of it. You did not ask for more enquiries. You asked for your week back. So this is not a lead generation document and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

🔴 The Single Biggest Finding, And It Explains The Forty Hours
🔴🔴 Your website has no enquiry form on it. Not one, on any page

We checked every page. There are zero contact forms on transwarmerconservatories.co.uk and nine separate links to your mobile number. Your “Get a Quote!” button does not open a form — it goes to your About and Contact page.

⭐⭐ So every single enquiry you receive, from every source, arrives as a phone call to 07931 340371. Which is your personal mobile, which is presumably in your pocket, which means:

  • You cannot be unavailable. If you miss the call there is no second route — no form sitting in an inbox waiting for you, and no email address a customer can be bothered to type.
  • Nobody else can handle an enquiry, because it does not exist anywhere except on your phone.
  • Enquiries arrive while you are working, on a roof or in a customer's conservatory, which is the worst possible moment to take one well.
  • Nothing is written down. No record of who called, what they wanted, what you quoted, or whether you followed up.

🔴 You asked how to work ten hours a week instead of forty. A business where every enquiry must be personally answered by the owner, by phone, cannot be run in ten hours. The structure forbids it.

⭐ And this is the most fixable thing on this page. It is a form, a routing rule and somewhere for enquiries to live. The hours page sets out the rest.

What Is Genuinely Good, Because Most Of It Is
⭐ You are far better positioned than most firms we audit
  • ⭐⭐ You are a specialist, not a general trade. Warm roof conversions for conservatories. One thing, done properly. Most of the businesses we look at are trying to be nine things at once, and you are not — which is a significant structural advantage and you should not give it up.
  • You lead with a named position: “Leicestershire's premier warm roof installer”. A place and a product. That is more than most manage.
  • Three clearly differentiated products — TapcoSlate, ExtraLight tiles and SkyVista glass panels — each with its own page. A customer can actually tell them apart.
  • A ten year guarantee, stated plainly.
  • Benefits written for the customer, not the trade — lower heating bills, cooler in summer, less rain noise, usable all year. ⭐ That is genuinely good copy and whoever wrote it understood the buyer.
  • You have Google Analytics installed. ⭐ You are ahead of most of this week's audits on that alone — three of the four businesses we looked at have no measurement at all.
  • A Recent Work page and a case study.
  • ⭐ “From the first time you contact us, you'll be treated with respect, instead of as a prospect.” That line is the best thing on your website. It is a real differentiator in a trade with a reputation problem, and it is buried a long way down the page.
The Rest Of The Problems
⚠️ 1. Analytics is installed but nothing is being measured that matters

You have Google Analytics, which records visits. ⚠️ But with no form to submit and no tracked click, there is no conversion to count. So you can see that people arrived and never that any of them became an enquiry.

⭐ Once a form exists this becomes genuinely useful, and the tool is already there. You are one component away from being able to see your own funnel.

⚠️ 2. Your mobile is the business number, with no landline anywhere

07931 340371 is the only telephone number published. For a business selling £10,000–£12,000 installations with a ten year guarantee, a mobile-only presence reads as a smaller and shorter-lived operation than thirty-five years of experience deserves.

⭐ You do have enquiries@ on your own domain, which is right. It is simply not where anybody is being sent.

⚠️ 3. Thirty-five years of experience is mentioned once, in passing, near the bottom

The About section says “Our business operates on family values, drawing from more than 35 years of experience.”

⭐⭐ Thirty-five years in a trade where firms appear and vanish inside three is your single strongest claim, and it is one clause in a paragraph most visitors will never reach. A customer worried about who will honour a ten year guarantee is asking exactly this question, and you are answering it quietly.

⚠️ 4. No accreditation or scheme appears anywhere on the site

We found no mention of FENSA, CERTASS, TrustMark, Checkatrade, or any insurance-backed guarantee provider.

⚠️ This matters more in glazing than in most trades, because the work touches Building Regulations — your own site says the system is “Building Regulations compliant”. A customer who has been told to check for a scheme and cannot find one on your site will ask a competitor instead.

⭐ If you hold any of these and they are simply not shown, that is a ten minute fix. We are asking, not assuming.

⚠️ 5. One to three clients a week, at the lower end, from referrals only

You told us the volume varies and tends to the bottom of that range. A business entirely dependent on word of mouth has no way to smooth that out — referrals arrive when they arrive, and a quiet fortnight is invisible until it has already happened.

⚠️ We are not raising this to sell you leads. We are raising it because a business you want to step back from needs predictable work, not just enough work, and referral-only is the least predictable source there is.

The Staffing Problem, And An Uncomfortable Possibility
⛔ We are not recruiters. But something in your brief is worth testing

You said your people are not committed and do not have the skillset, and that it has been that way for years. ⭐ “For years” is the important part of that sentence. A run of bad luck with individuals looks like a hiring problem. The same complaint for several years, through several people, is usually structural.

⚠️ So the question we would want to ask: are these people employed by you, or are they subcontractors?

Because if they are subcontractors, the behaviour you are describing is exactly what the arrangement produces, and no amount of recruiting changes it:

  • A subcontractor has other customers, and yours is one of them. Commitment is not the deal.
  • They arrive with the skills they already have. Nobody is training them in your systems, because nobody is paying for that time.
  • They have no reason to care about your guarantee in year eight.

⭐⭐ If that is the shape of it, “three more quality committed people” is not a recruitment brief — it is a decision about employing people. That is a significant and expensive change, it is entirely yours to make, and it is well outside what a marketing company should be advising on. But we would rather name it than sell you advertising for a problem advertising cannot reach.

⛔ And if they are already employed, this paragraph is wrong and we would want to hear that on Sunday.

What We Could Not Verify
Said plainly
  • Whether you hold FENSA, CERTASS or any similar registration. None appears on the site.
  • Whether your three to five people are employed or subcontracted. This changes the whole conversation and we cannot tell from outside.
  • How the thirty-five years relates to the current company. Transwarmer Conservatories Ltd was incorporated in May 2021, so the experience plainly predates it. ⭐ That is completely normal and not a criticism — we simply do not know how the business was structured before, and it affects what we could honestly claim on your behalf.
  • What your enquiry volume actually is, as opposed to your job volume. With no form and no call tracking, nobody knows — including you.
  • What your conversion rate is. Same reason.
  • Whether a ten hour week is actually achievable given your role on site. ⚠️ If you are fitting roofs yourself, that is a different problem from if you are quoting and managing.
What We Would Want To Ask On Sunday
  • 1. Are you on the tools? ⭐ The most important question on the page. If you are fitting, ten hours means replacing yourself as a fitter. If you are quoting and managing, it means replacing yourself as an office.
  • 2. Employed or subcontract? See above.
  • 3. Walk us through a normal week. Where do the forty hours actually go — quoting, surveying, fitting, chasing, invoicing, answering that mobile?
  • 4. How many calls a week, roughly, and how many become jobs? Even a rough number. Nobody is currently counting.
  • 5. Do you hold FENSA, CERTASS or an insurance-backed guarantee? If so it is missing from your site.
  • 6. Who could answer the phone instead of you? Anybody at all — family, a part-timer, a service?
  • 7. If you got to ten hours a week, what happens to the business? ⚠️ Does it keep running at this size, or does somebody else have to run it? Those are very different plans.
Question one decides which of two completely different conversations we are having. We would rather establish it in the first two minutes than build a plan on the wrong one.

Nine links to your mobile. Zero enquiry forms. One person able to take a job.

That is not a marketing problem. It is the reason the week is forty hours.
