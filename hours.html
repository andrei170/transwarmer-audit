Getting Your Week Back, In The Order It Would Have To Happen

Prepared for Andrew Perridge · 4 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
Where Do The Forty Hours Go?

Left blank on purpose. We do not know your week and guessing at it would be the fastest way to build the wrong plan. ⭐ Filling this in is the first ten minutes of Sunday's call.

 Where the week goesHoursNeeds you?
AAnswering the phone and taking enquiries——
BSurveying and measuring at the customer's house——
CPricing and writing the quote up——
DChasing quotes that have gone quiet——
EOn the tools, fitting——
FManaging the fitters, scheduling, ordering materials——
GInvoicing, paperwork, books, chasing payment——
HGoing back to sort something out after the job——
Row E is the one that decides everything. ⭐ If you are fitting roofs yourself for most of the week, then getting to ten hours means somebody else has to fit roofs, and that is a hiring and training problem that no system touches. If row E is small and the hours are really in A, C, D, F and G, then ten hours is genuinely reachable — because that is all process, and process can be moved off a person.
What Can Come Off You, And What Cannot
⭐ Can be taken off you without hiring anybody
  • Receiving the enquiry. ⭐ You already have this on four pages — it is simply missing from your homepage and your Recent Work page, which are the two most people see.
  • Acknowledging it. An automatic reply the moment it lands, so the customer knows they have reached a real business before you have looked at it. ⭐ This is the gap, not the form.
  • Qualifying it. The form asks the size, the type of conservatory, the postcode and roughly when — so you know before you ring whether it is worth a visit.
  • Remembering it. Every enquiry in one list with its status, instead of in your call log and your head.
  • Booking the survey. Letting them pick a slot you have made available, rather than three calls back and forth.
  • Chasing the quote. ⭐ On a timer, automatically. This is almost always the biggest single source of recovered revenue in a business like yours, and it costs you nothing once it exists.
  • Producing the quote document — your prices and specification into a template that formats and sends it. The pricing stays yours; the typing does not.
  • Invoicing and payment chasing.
⛔ Cannot, and we will not pretend otherwise
  • Fitting the roof. Somebody competent has to be on it.
  • The survey itself. Somebody has to look at the existing conservatory and know what they are looking at. ⚠️ This is the hardest one to delegate and it may be the real ceiling on your ten hours.
  • Pricing a difficult job. Thirty-five years of judgement is not a template.
  • Deciding which jobs to decline.
  • The relationship on a job that has gone wrong. ⭐ A ten year guarantee means your name is on it, and a customer with a problem wants the owner.

⚠️ Be honest with yourself about the right-hand column. If most of your forty hours is in it, the answer is people, not systems — and we would tell you that rather than sell you software that cannot help.

The Order It Would Have To Happen In
  • 1. Fix the broken heading and put a form on the homepage. ⭐ Both are small. The heading bug is minutes, and the form already exists on four other pages — it just needs to be where people land. Your Recent Work page needs one too.
  • 2. Give enquiries somewhere to live. One list, every enquiry, with a status, whether it arrived by form or by phone. ⚠️ This is the real gap. A spreadsheet would be a genuine improvement on the current position, so it does not have to be expensive to start.
  • 3. Automatic acknowledgement on arrival. ⭐ The cheapest competitive advantage available to you — most of your competitors do not do it, and the customer who hears back first disproportionately wins the job. Your forms currently collect an enquiry; nothing replies to it.
  • 4. Wire the forms to the analytics you already have. ⭐ You have Google Analytics and four forms, and no record of a single submission. Connecting the two costs nothing and you would see your own funnel for the first time.
  • 5. Automatic follow-up on quiet quotes. On a timer, no memory required.
  • 6. Take the phone off your personal mobile — or at least put a second route beside it, so being unavailable stops costing you the enquiry.
  • 7. Only then look at where more work comes from. ⛔ Advertising into the current setup would make your week worse, not better, because every extra enquiry is another call to your pocket. We would decline to run it in that order.
⭐ Note that steps one to six do not generate a single extra enquiry. They capture, keep and convert the ones you already get, and they take work off you while doing it. For somebody trying to reduce their hours, that is the correct order, and it is the opposite of what most agencies would sell you.
The Part Of This That Is Not Ours
⛔ Two honest limits on how far we can take you

First: we cannot get you to ten hours if you are a fitter. Everything above removes office and enquiry work. If the bulk of your week is on a roof, then the ceiling on this is roughly however many hours you currently spend not fitting — and the rest is a hiring and training question.

Second: a business that runs without you still needs somebody to run it. ⚠️ At one to ten hours a week you are not managing day to day, which means either the business stays this size and largely runs itself on process, or somebody else is managing it. Those are two very different futures and the second one is a succession conversation, not a marketing one.

⭐ We raise both because a plan that ignores them would be a plan that fails in month four, and you would reasonably blame us for it.

Why This Connects To The Staffing Complaint
⭐ The two problems are closer than they look

You said your people lack commitment and the right skillset. ⭐ Consider what a new fitter currently walks into: no written process, no system, no record of what was quoted or promised, and an owner who holds all of it in his head and on his phone.

In that environment, even a good person looks unreliable, because there is nothing for them to be reliable against. Every job depends on you remembering to tell them something.

⭐⭐ So the systems work above is also the groundwork for the hiring you want. A business with a written process can absorb a new person. A business that runs out of one man's memory cannot, however good the recruit. Build the first and the second gets easier.

⛔ That is not a promise that it solves your staffing. It is an argument that the order matters: systems before people, because people cannot be dropped into an absence of systems.

Steps one to six do not bring you one extra enquiry.

They are the only ones that give you your week back.
